# Fieldwork naming and world identity

**Status:** Adopted working standard v1.0 (2026-11-27). Revisit as the game evolves. This is a living creative contract, not a claim of historical verification or commercial clearance.

## Core principle

Abstract reality, don't anonymize it. Preserve defining identity and remove redundant detail, just as in Fieldwork's visual art. Recognition takes precedence over rigid formatting; specificity must be earned by the actual model or historical reference. Authenticity is not endorsement.

## World and historical context

- Real countries, regions, nationalities and appropriate national flag patches are permitted. Prefer fictional sites, addresses, characters, organizations and incidents in authored contemporary campaigns; real cities can establish context without recreating actual incidents.
- Real historical units, organizations, dates, places and wars may appear in reference/loadout libraries and historical settings. Fictional operations within real historical settings are permitted; authored scenarios are fictional by default. Do not present a representative loadout as a verified exact reconstruction. Use exact years only with evidence, otherwise ranges or decades.
- Fieldwork is a flexible anthology, not one mandatory fictional geopolitical canon. Dispatch may develop a persistent fictional city/department; Warzone/Raid may vary era and region; Wasteland/Survival may have its own continuity. Sandbox allows player-defined identities.
- Minimize contemporary political affiliations, real political figures and slogans; avoid real extremist branding, sensationalizing real tragedies, and national or religious stereotypes. Do not require invented nations or erase relevant historical context.

## Equipment

- Use the common recognizable model name: M4A1, AK-74, FAL, FNC, G36K, HK416, CZ 75, Glock 19, Remington 870, Mossberg 500, Uzi. Keep manufacturer names when integral to recognition; omit gratuitous prefixes and marketing copy. Preserve established punctuation and authentic variants.
- Use iconic attachment designations (e.g. SOPMOD Stock) when the modeled identity supports them; use meaningful descriptive names (Tube Red Dot, Compact Light, Two-Point Sling) for representative archetypes. Keep an inspiration/style qualifier for approximate models rather than asserting exact product identity.
- Apply the same distinction to armor, helmets, webbing, packs, clothing and footwear. PASGT Helmet, ACH Helmet, ALICE Webbing and M65 Field Jacket are appropriate for specifically represented equipment; High-Cut Helmet, Assault Pack, Hiking Boots and Lightweight Plate Carrier are appropriate archetypes. Do not invent model precision.
- Preserve established camouflage pattern identities and meaningful variant suffixes. Use one shared vocabulary for equivalent solid colors across categories. Commercial pattern names are subject to the same review as other product names.
- No gratuitous manufacturer logos or marketing. Real names, marks and designs may require separate legal review before commercial release; this policy is not a determination of rights.

## Editorial rules

Use title case for catalog items and sentence case for action labels. Keep recognized acronyms without periods; prefer clear interface language (Extraction, Medical Kit) over unnecessary jargon, while authentic dialogue can use shorthand. Use numerals for established model designations, quantities, measurements, dates, callsigns and real standardized classifications; use descriptive words for physical differences rather than invented Mk tiers or arbitrary numbers. Keep historically meaningful Mk/Mod designations. Put category, caliber, era, origin, capacity, color and mounting details in existing metadata where available instead of overloading the name. Use concise incident descriptions for Dispatch, fictional operation codenames where useful for Warzone/Raid, and practical objective descriptions for Survival.

## Application and compatibility

Canonical display names should agree across equipment, arsenal, loadouts, historical references, labs, inventory and UI wherever they refer to the same identity. Preserve reference descriptions and evidence. Do not rename stable IDs, storage keys, compatibility keys, geometry names or user-authored preset names to enforce editorial style. Do not change mechanics, inventory or historical contents as part of a label-only pass. Avoid broad search-and-replace; inspect each authoritative catalog and its consumers, including saved-loadout compatibility. Review genuinely ambiguous identities case by case; no blanket brand stripping or unsupported specificity.

Apply prospectively and normalize existing labels in small compatible release batches using existing tests and release gates. No separate naming framework is required where an authoritative catalog already exists.
