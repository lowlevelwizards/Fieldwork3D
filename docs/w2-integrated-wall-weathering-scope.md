# W2 — Architectural surface redesign and rendering compiler pilot

Status: revised scope, not implemented/promoted. Base: 6e1cd300305a9100f33b6463f2fcf93a5b688591 (merged W0–W1). Exact Pages release still unverified as of this scope revision; do not promote W2 before verified deployment. User direction: completely rethink actual visuals AND drawing process, not just optimize existing dirt overlays. This supersedes the original shader-only integration proposal.

## Design thesis: architecture as a finished object, not stacked decoration

Generate a legible architectural surface from building semantics, then compile that surface into the fewest submissions compatible with independent knowledge and cutaway ownership. Geometry describes silhouette, thickness, actual openings and other visible physical features. Color, finish, mortar, joints, paint bands, baseboards, surface deterioration and graphic edge definition primarily belong to a surface recipe. Light and disclosure are presentation stages, not new copies of geometry. Keep the established Fieldwork chunky, simplified, low-detail iconic art style; not photorealistic procedural grunge. Make a compelling room/building at the normal tactical zoom first; close-up detail is secondary. Do not merely reproduce the old patina at lower draw cost.

## W2.1 — Art direction and executable auditions

Develop a render-only, pure deterministic wall surface recipe with material family, physical wall coordinates, base/secondary palette, finish/condition, broad face orientation and semantic anchors; no dependency on current camera or fog. Initial families: painted plaster/drywall (large calm fields, optional lower paint band), masonry (few strong courses and broken joints rather than busy uniform brick), industrial panel/concrete (large panel rhythm, seams and restrained utilitarian marks). Exterior versus interior can initially share authored palettes and existing segment-level exposure, but the schema must support two distinct faces without a new architecture; directional exposure and atlas scale in W3. Give cap/skirting colors a coherent relationship to wall body. Use a small shared palette, large shape language, physically scaled pixel motifs; flat/clean large areas dominate. Keep high-contrast marks intentional: no arbitrary noisy brown overlay, no ubiquitous cracks or stains, no glossy realism. Weathering selects a few meaningful patches: abrasion near threshold/traffic, damp at floor, runoff directly below windows, occasional chips/cracks only when age justifies it. Use seed for variation, not per-frame random. Correct top-down orientation explicitly; old DataTexture v=0 top data vs old mesh v=0 bottom needs witness. Avoid double aging by jointly choosing base tint and wear. Distinguish materials at 0/25/50/75/100 condition; do not make all families brown.

Create an executable in-engine wall audition (Map Lab or existing renderer preview entry, no unrelated menu): same camera and light, clean / worn / neglected, house/retail or clinic/garage or warehouse; front/rear, short/long, junction, opening, interior/exterior and day/night. Compare W1 legacy beside candidate and optionally two candidate surface realizations. Actual browser screenshots and wall crops at tactical and close inspection, not concept art alone. Choose visual treatment before generalizing. Keep local decals only where real semantic marks are needed; prototype whether any static mark can be encoded into the surface without loss, but do not remove interactive VFX or independent openings. Establish visual approval checkpoint before production default; artifact access or missing subjective review must be explicit.

## W2.2 — Surface compiler and drawing process pilot

Create a single render-only surface descriptor/compilation boundary between generated site semantics and Three.js. Keep authoring, compilation, presentation and resource lifetime separate.
- Author: deterministic segment/face finish recipe, semantic anchor coordinates (floor, edges, window opening), material/condition.
- Compile: output low-resolution surface data and a compact render packet; one body submission per visible segment for now, using existing body geometry or equivalently sized geometry, no extra weather geometry or transparent weather material. Prefer existing shared base pixel textures with stable metric UV plus compact shared/masked surface data. Compare a baked low-resolution RGB(A) surface approach (single fetch, potential texture cost) and shader composition (shared tile plus compact mask, extra fragment fetch) using representative same-scene browser evidence; do not ship both as permanent competing systems. A shared material recipe may encode motifs and localized deterministic wear; a wall-specific texture is not the target end state. Choose based on actual visual quality, draw/program counts, cold uploads, steady cost, memory and W3/W4 compatibility. A bounded pilot with per-segment mask is allowed if it removes overlays and has a clear atlas migration path. Reuse existing pixel-recipe/condition helpers only where they support revised art; do not preserve a failing art algorithm just because it exists.
- Use physical-space coordinates independent of repeating tile UVs. Broad faces sample correct orientation and top-down height; thin edges/cap/skirting not accidentally stained. Prefer no per-wall geometry clones and no mutation of shared geometry, map transforms or palette caches. Stable shader keys, no per-wall program variants or per-fragment complex noise loops. Make all color operations linear-space correct; weather is surface color/finish, not opacity or emissive film. Retain graphic lighting hooks and legacy light mode, shadow eligibility, NVG/flashlights and VFX interactions.
- Present: W1 segment ownership remains the authoritative wall fade; preserve parent disclosure and independently resolved opacity/depthWrite/transparency. Normal walls render opaque except existing known-facade pass policy, cutaway fades entire surface together. Existing fog order 1000/1001/1002 retained until W5. No new independent visibility objects, no per-frame recipe generation, no repeated overlay traversal. No fog state, camera or gameplay data embedded into cached materials.
- Geometry: pilot body only, but specify final W4 structural packet with body/skirting/cap/junction visually coherent in compact mesh when possible; use painted graphic trim instead of separate solid geometry if silhouette and close camera do not require it. Preserve actual physical thickness, opening frames and collision. W2 may replace a decorative body-only overlay/mark with surface detail, not rewrite whole scene batching. W4 must preserve per-segment presentation and may group only compatible states/disclosure. Do not merge unseen with known interiors for draw-count savings.

## W2.3 — Lifecycle and integrated art testing

One renderer-owned site surface registry, existing sitePatinaTextures/patinaWallSurfaces or deliberate replacement rather than a parallel production system. No duplicate allocations, duplicate unique albedo textures without evidence, texture size bounded (reference 64–256 x 64 RGBA8+ mip per weathered segment), no new render targets. Shared resources must survive site swap; site resources disposed exactly once. Map Lab slider coalesces updates, modifies existing buffers at 0–100–0, deterministic no geometry/program churn; initial clean site must become weathered when preview commits, no unnecessary gameplay allocations. Old floor weathering untouched. Legacy path available during controlled A/B via ?wallWear=legacy, opt-in candidate ?wallWear=integrated until acceptance. Remove old production overlays when promoted; keep minimal rollback until W3 consolidation, not two permanent pipelines. Preserve local surface wear/impacts/blood/bullet marks as independent dynamic evidence; no dynamic texture rebake each frame.

## Revised downstream roadmap (design boundaries, not implementation yet)

W3: shared surface atlas/library, face-specific interior/exterior finishes and directional exposure, reuse tiles/palette/mask data, packed face coordinates and seam-safe mip gutters. 
W4: architecture mesh compiler: body/edge/trim simplification, structural joints, grouped material/geometry and compatible visibility state; reduce draw calls aggressively without compromising structural silhouette, openings or disclosure. 
W5: replace transparent facade restoration workaround with a coherent opaque depth/disclosure/fog architecture, preserving tactical, photo and replay semantics.
W6: architectural/environmental storytelling from site semantics, condition, use and climate; sparse purposeful surface accents. 
W7: apply proven surface compilation to floors and static fixtures, integrate baked static wear where appropriate, not dynamic damage.
W8: combined visual quality review, mobile and desktop measured acceptance, regression and deployment; goal >=60% structural wall draw reduction across W roadmap, not W2 guarantee.

Out of scope in W2: wholesale gameplay renderer replacement, global batching across disclosure ownership, simulation/worldgen changes, new floors/props or lighting systems, premature W3–W8 implementation. The new surface compiler should make those stages natural extensions rather than lock in existing visual defects.
