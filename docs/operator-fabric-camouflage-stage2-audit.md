# Fieldwork / Soldier Appearance Stage 2 — camouflage application audit

**Audit date:** 2026-11-03  
**Scope:** The 30 approved Camouflage 5B patterns, not the old generic procedural
17-pattern wardrobe collection. The original pixels, palettes, density recipes,
headwear eligibility and headwear model fit remain frozen.

## Decision standard

A print must have an actual military-issue example, or a credible commercially
made equivalent, *on the kind of cloth or equipment being selected*. A camouflage
pattern's existence on a jacket does not justify a matching plate carrier, every
style of backpack, weapon, boots, rubberized knee pad, plastic buckle, or eyewear.

Decisions below are intentionally **conservative**. "Eligible" means there is a
credible historically fielded or manufactured class of object. It does **not**
mean every individual available Fieldwork silhouette or exact production
year is identical; style-family guards further restrict vintage vs. modern
cuts. A printed commercial tactical nylon item is not presented as military
issue. Where the specific item could not be substantiated, we leave it solid.

The pattern image itself is a stylized, original Fieldwork reproduction.
Country-issued names here identify visual reference families, not a claim
to reproduce exact government textile specifications or trademarks.

**Keys:** U = jacket/field blouse, **T** = trousers, **A** = body-armor
textile shell/cover, **G** = eligible textile webbing, rig panels or pouches,
**P** = backpack outer fabric. The separately approved headwear eligibility
matrix stays authoritative for **H**, including its existing allowed cuts.
An unlisted class is **not offered**. No camouflage paint on rigid plates,
boots, lenses, radios, buckles, weapon bodies or exposed skin.

### More precise style guards

- **Tops:** Only field jackets, BDU/ACU-style blouses, combat shirts,
  field shirts, and print-appropriate tactical outerwear. Civilian T-shirts,
  flannel shirts, sweaters, tank tops and base layers don't receive these
  uniform selections merely because their textile could technically be printed.
  Historical prints generally use historic field cuts. Alpenflage and
  Splittertarn 31 are limited to the current field-jacket approximation until
  more truthful silhouette models are built in Stage 3.
- **Trousers:** Field, BDU, ACU and modern combat trousers where documented;
  older period patterns are limited to field/BDU cuts. No jeans, civilian shorts,
  track pants, base shorts or boots. Splittertarn trousers represent the
  documented *limited winter ensemble*, not an everyday matching BDU.
- **Body armor:** The camo colors only the outer textile. Steel/ceramic plate
  geometry, rubber closure, plastic buckle and non-fabric pieces remain solid.
  Camo cannot apply if armor isn't equipped. Accepted patterns are restricted
  to a pattern with an actual armor textile cover/carrier precedent.
- **Webbing/rig:** Rigs require actual kit; no camo when 'no-rig'. East German
  Strichtarn is limited to the field-webbing/belt-harness class, and British DPM
  to plausible field/PLCE-style load carriage, not micro rigs by assumption.
- **Packs:** Empty/no-pack remains bare. Alpenflage, Strichtarn and woodland
  MARPAT use the field-pack/rucksack class (Alpenflage vintage, NVA vintage,
  and MARPAT ILBE respectively). Other supported prints require a made-to-match
  textile object, not painting an untextured hard pack.
- **Headgear:** All seven headgear families, historically restricted 30-print
  choices, solid swatches, seeded cuts and palette remain governed by the
  already approved Camouflage 5B headwear implementation. No merge or changes.

### Saved outfits and mission rules

- Store the five new fabric channels separately from older 17 procedural pattern
  IDs; a historic 'flecktarn' procedural texture is not silently rewritten to
  the newly approved authored 'flecktarn' art.
- A fabric selection is associated with its **garment channel**, and its
  phase/roll is saved independently. Four operators with the same M81 do not
  have identical cuts. Rerolling the jacket does not reroll trousers or headgear.
- If an outfit or actual purchased kit no longer supports a saved print,
  preserve the saved authored selection but **do not display that print**.
  The selector explains the incompatibility instead of changing protection,
  magically supplying equipment, or erasing personal data.
- Lab looks, campaign personnel and Quick Play cosmetics serialize optional
  fields through the existing v1 wardrobe parser. Quick Play still discards
  health, kit and supplies between rounds, *not* cosmetic selections.
- The old 17 procedural patterns stay readable/selectable. Picking one again
  explicitly clears any authored print override in that same channel.
- Native 32/64px art uses one pattern-specific real-world density, nearest
  sampling, and consistent sewn-panel box UV offsets. Metal/plastic/rubber and
  the frozen headwear atlas are not transformed.

### Audit limitations, explicit follow-ups

This is a conservative **category** matrix, not yet a photographed proof of
every print on every manufacturer/model. Some international pattern/gear
combinations (notably Polish Pantera, Italian Vegetato, older Flora, Belgian
Jigsaw and Swedish M90 aftermarket items) have less direct primary-source
manufacturer documentation. Their gear permissions represent issued or
credible available soft-goods families, not a guarantee that a specific modern
plate carrier was deployed by that nation's army. Stage 3's replacement
models should refine the policy to **per physical SKU/model** rather than
widen these permissions. Never infer a material-print use from the mere fact
that a pattern appears in the game's catalog.
