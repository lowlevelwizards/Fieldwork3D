# Fieldwork 3D — Environmental Art Standard

## The core rule
Readability comes from a physically convincing silhouette before small details or procedural texture. At tactical zoom every prop should communicate its identity, material and functional orientation.

## Construction priorities
1. Primary volume: broad, stubby, deliberate low-poly masses, not inflated cubes.
2. Structural load path: connected legs, hinges, frames, wheels, braces and mounted controls.
3. Material hierarchy: painted metal, steel, rubber, plastic, fabric, porcelain, foliage and wood have distinct structure and wear.
4. Purposeful character: family-specific proportions and seeded logical variants.
5. History: small, material-aware traces of use, exposure and maintenance—never random noise across the entire surface.

## Rendering budgets
- Preserve canonical 20 world units/meter and authoritative collision/LOS.
- Prefer geometry and material reuse; keep normal props inexpensive at phone tactical zoom.
- Use shadows only where form and contact benefit.
- Never make close-detail geometry a requirement for recognition.
- Stable seeded art variations should survive replay and map regeneration.

## Live art review
- Capture all Asset Library entries from iso, opposite and overhead angles at controlled size.
- Inspect both focused close-up and actual tactical distance.
- Audit against silhouette, construction, proportions, material, personality and tactical readability.
- The register intentionally marks unreviewed objects as such. Generated contact sheets cannot automatically approve human visual quality.
- Resolve incorrect attachments, mismatched proportions, material errors and distant readability regressions before approval.

## Integration
The Asset Library and campaign share the same geometry constructors.
The showroom may add cosmetic preview metadata only, never alter tactical rules.
The full catalog geometry gate checks model existence; human art approval remains a distinct task.
